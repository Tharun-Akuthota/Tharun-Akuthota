How to do the warm-up and cool-down

5-minute warm-up

  1. Easy march — 60 seconds
  2. Shoulder circles — 10 each way
  3. Hip hinges — 8 slow reps
  4. Alternating heel taps — 60 seconds
  5. Easy chair sit-to-stands — 5 reps

3–5-minute cool-down

  1. Slow walk until breathing settles
  2. Calf stretch — 20 sec per side
  3. Chest stretch — 20 sec per side
  4. Gentle hip-flexor stretch — 20 sec per side
  5. Relaxed breathing — 5 slow breaths

Technique and safety

  • Exhale during the effort; do not hold breath.
  • Stop for sharp pain, chest pain, faintness or unusual breathlessness.
  • Use stable furniture; never anchor bands to a loose door handle.
  • Muscle effort is okay; joint pain is a signal to stop or modify.

Portion guide and simple nutrition rules

At lunch and dinner

  • About ½ plate vegetables/salad.
  • About ¼ plate protein-rich food.
  • About ¼ plate rice, roti or another grain/starchy food.
  • Adjust portions for hunger, activity and progress.

Protein through the day

Include a protein source at each main meal: eggs, chicken, fish, milk, curd, dal, beans, soy chunks, tofu or paneer. A practical starting range for many healthy adults in this situation may be around 80–100 g/day, but needs vary. Do not use this target if a clinician has prescribed protein restriction.

Fluids

  • Keep water accessible during duty.
  • Use water, buttermilk, milk or unsweetened soy milk as useful options.
  • Limit sugary drinks and frequent high-calorie coffees.
  • Liquid calories count too; smoothies are optional, not mandatory.

Flexible hospital-day backups

  • Boiled eggs + fruit
  • Roasted chana + water
  • Plain curd/yogurt
  • Milk or soy milk
  • Peanut/seed portion + fruit
  • Leftover chapati roll with egg, chicken or tofu

Meal timing for rotating shifts

SituationSimple approach
Regular day shiftBreakfast after waking; lunch during a break; portable snack mid-shift; dinner after work. Move times to fit duties.
Early startIf there is no time for a full breakfast, take a banana and milk/curd or eggs/toast, then eat a fuller meal at the first practical break.
Night shiftHave a balanced meal before the shift, pack 1–2 snacks and a meal, drink water, and have a lighter meal if hungry after work. Avoid relying on sweets and energy drinks to stay alert.
Very busy / missed mealEat a practical snack when possible and return to the usual plan at the next meal. Do not punish herself by fasting or doing extra exercise.

Daily checklist and weekly review

Daily checklist

Weekly check-in

  • Weight: once a week, same conditions; compare trends over 3–4 weeks.
  • Waist: every 2–4 weeks, same measuring point and posture.
  • Strength: note reps and band/backpack used.
  • Energy, hunger, sleep, soreness and menstrual regularity.
  • Do not use daily scale changes to judge success.
